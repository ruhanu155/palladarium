# Concept ideas

**Status: ideas to pick from.** Nothing here is in the plan until Ruhan chooses it. Each idea lists the pillar it serves, how it plays, its rough build size (S, M or L), and the version it would fit.

**Top 5 picks**, with the reason for each:

1. **Time-lapse cameras and the restoration film.** It makes "the land answers back" visible across years, and it is the best payoff in the game.
2. **The field dog.** A calm companion that is also a real survey tool, and it makes the world feel warmer.
3. **Field moments.** A collection of animal behaviors to witness, which turns patience into play.
4. **The cove's history.** Found records tell why the land is damaged, so restoring it means something.
5. **Catch-and-release angling surveys.** A relaxing hands-on verb that doubles as science.

## Big concepts

**1. Time-lapse cameras and the restoration film.** *Pillars: the land answers back; grow without an end. Size M. Fits v0.5.*

- The player mounts up to 6 time-lapse cameras on posts aimed at chosen views, such as the gully, Miller Pond and Bench Field.
- Each takes one frame every game morning.
- At any time, the journal plays a camera's frames as a short film: the gully going green, the pond filling, the meadow flowering.
- At the finale, the frames from all cameras play as the restoration film.
- Players will place cameras before big projects just to see the change later. In Minecraft, a frame is a small server-side render of the view, or a stored terrain snapshot replayed in the client.

**2. The field dog.** *Pillars: hands-on; real detail. Adopted into the plan: see the Tame and working animals tab.*

- A trained detection dog, like the real dogs used to find box turtles and rare plants.
- Commands: *heel*, *search* (pick a target: box turtle, salamander, or the scat of a named mammal), *stay*, *quiet*.
- On a search, the dog quarters the ground and sits when it finds the scent. The player confirms the find and logs it.
- The dog boosts survey effort for those targets. Off its lead near nests, it scares wildlife and costs reputation, so a lead (an item) matters.
- It sleeps in the RV, greets you at the door, and never chases, catches or harms wildlife.

**3. Field moments.** *Pillars: calm but not static; real detail. Size M. Fits v0.3.*

- A collection of real behaviors to witness:
  - a heron spearing a fish
  - a wood duck brood jumping from the box
  - a box turtle laying eggs
  - trout rising to a hatch
  - woodcock in the sky dance
  - a bear stripping blackberries
  - fireflies syncing
  - a hellbender guarding eggs
- Each moment needs the right place, season and time of day, and a quiet player.
- Seeing one logs it with a photo slot and a short line of fact. About 40 moments in the cove.
- It rewards patience and teaches behavior without a single quest. Moments use the behavior goals mobs already have, plus a trigger that notices when the player is watching.

**4. The cove's history.** *Pillars: real detail; everything readable. Size S. Fits v0.3.*

About 15 found records tell why the cove looks the way it does. They are written plainly, with no invented heroes, and each found record adds a page to the Chronicle's prologue. Each record also points at a real thing on the land:

- a 1910s logging company receipt in the Homeplace
- a 1930s letter about the chestnuts dying
- a 1950s farm pond permit
- an old survey map with the witness tree
- a photograph of the cove clear-cut

**5. Catch-and-release angling surveys.** *Pillars: hands-on; calm. Size M. Fits v0.3.*

- A barbless-hook rod for the Run and Miller Pond. Casting, a quiet wait and the strike are simple to learn, and playing the fish gently keeps its stress low.
- Each fish is measured, then released. Angling counts toward fish survey effort and the biggest-trout line on the year card.
- Rules: barbless hooks only; wet hands; release within the stress timer. No angling in the Run's spawning season.
- It is a relaxing verb many Minecraft players already love, turned into science.

**6. Guided walks.** *Pillars: grow without an end. Size M. Fits v0.5.*

- School groups or visitors book a walk through the orders board.
- The player leads them along a route of chosen stops (the pond, the Run, the meadow) and shows them something live at each: a kick-net tray, a frog, a nest box.
- The group reacts at each stop, and the walk pays reputation and sometimes orders.
- Calm social play without villages. It gives a reason to keep showpiece spots beautiful.

**7. Scenario mode.** *Pillars: respect the player's time. Size M. Fits after v1.0.*

- Short, self-contained challenges on small plots, each 1–2 game seasons long:
  - "Stop this gully before the spring storms"
  - "Keep this pond alive through a drought"
  - "Get trout back above a culvert"
- Each is scored on the outcome, with 3 marks per scenario.
- Gives short sessions and replay value, and reuses every system as-is.

## Round 2 ideas (October 19, 2026)

New ideas checked against every tab, so none of them repeats something already planned. Adopted October 19, 2026: Ruhan picked all 13. Each one is specified in the tab named in the release table (Fun, feel and finish → Path to a finished game); this section keeps the original pitch.

**Top 5 of this round**, with the reason for each:

1. **Mystery records.** Evidence before the animal turns surveys into detective work: play, don't follow.
2. **Tags that travel.** The cove connects to the wider world without any travel.
3. **Trail craft.** A hands-on build system where the land answers back: bad trails gully, good ones last.
4. **Beaver dam analogs.** A real, cheap restoration technique that reshapes a channel and can bring beavers.
5. **Dark sky.** A night-time resource to protect, with calm rewards.

**8. Mystery records.** *Pillars: play, don't follow; everything readable. Size M. Fits v0.4.*

- Sometimes the evidence comes before the animal: an eDNA hit for a hellbender in a reach where none has been seen, a bat call on the detector that matches no logged species, a track in the mud, a feather at the pond.
- The journal opens a mystery card with the clue and what it rules out. Each new survey in the right place adds a clue: season, time of day, microhabitat, size.
- The player narrows it down and confirms it with a sighting, a photo or a capture. A solved mystery pays double the first-sighting bonus and 5 science credits.
- In the sim, a mystery is a population with N above 0 that no survey has detected yet, plus a list of clue rules for that species. About 12 mysteries in the cove.

**9. Tags that travel.** *Pillars: the land answers back; grow without an end. Size S. Fits v0.5.*

- Animals tagged on the plot leave and sometimes report back, with real rates:
  - **Monarch:** "Monarch A-1182, tagged on Bench Field, was found in Michoacán, Mexico." About 1 tag in 100 is recovered.
  - **Louisiana waterthrush:** a banded bird returns to the same reach the next spring about half the time.
  - **Brook trout:** a PIT-tagged trout turns up downstream after the culvert is fixed.
- Reports arrive as postcards on the RV computer. The year card gets a "Returns" line, and a map in the journal shows where the cove's animals went.
- No entity ever leaves the plot. A report is a roll in the population sim when a tagged animal migrates or moves out.

**10. Trail craft.** *Pillars: the land answers back; hands-on. Size M. Fits v0.4.*

- Build a footpath network with real trail techniques. Waterbars turn runoff off the tread, stone steps climb steep pitches, switchbacks cut the grade, and a puncheon (a small log walkway) crosses wet spots.
- A trail steeper than 1 in 8 without waterbars starts to gully under the existing trail-wear rule. A well-built trail lasts, gives +20% walking speed, and keeps feet off the habitats beside it.
- Guided walks and visitors use the trails, so a good network matters for showing the cove.
- In Minecraft, finished tread is a packed-path block. A waterbar is a block that sends the column flow model's runoff sideways.

**11. Beaver dam analogs.** *Pillars: the land answers back; real detail. Size M. Fits v0.3.*

- A real low-cost restoration method. Drive a row of posts across a small cut-down channel and weave hazel alder or willow between them.
- The structure slows water, traps silt, raises the water table and turns the gully floor into wet meadow within a season.
- Storms damage it, so it needs a quick patch after each big rain. If beavers arrive, they may take it over and build it up themselves.
- It is allowed only on the gully and side channels, or on the Run with a 1-block notch, so trout can still pass.

**12. Dark sky.** *Pillars: calm but not static; everything readable. Size S. Fits v0.4.*

- The cove's night sky is a resource. Every outdoor light lowers a dark-sky score, warm white less than cool white and shielded lights least of all.
- Moths and fireflies already react to light. A high score adds Milky Way nights, the two big yearly meteor showers (one in summer, one in winter) and a "dark-sky site" badge.
- Lying on a blanket and watching the sky is a rest activity. In Comfort mode it restores a little energy.

**13. BioBlitz.** *Pillars: calm but not static; grow without an end. Size M. Fits v0.5.*

- Once a year, on the first day of summer, a 24-game-hour count of everything on the plot.
- The player sets up stations ahead of time: a moth sheet, a kick-net, a camera, a call point. Volunteers arrive by van, staff the stations, add their counts, and leave when it ends. They are visitors like the guided-walk groups, not a village.
- The species total goes on the year card next to last year's. A record total pays reputation and science credits.

**14. Heirloom orchard.** *Pillars: real detail; grow without an end. Size M. Fits v0.4.*

- The old homeplace apple is the start. Cut scion wood in winter, graft it onto rootstock at the workbench, then plant it out.
- Old Southern Appalachian apple varieties can be added through orders. The region once grew well over a thousand of them.
- The orchard feeds bears, deer and pollinators. Apples count as meals in the food and sleep option and can be sold through orders.
- Old trees need pruning each winter, or they bear less.

**15. The old-timer.** *Pillars: real detail; play, don't follow. Size S. Fits v0.5.*

- A retired neighbor who farmed the cove as a boy drives up once a season and walks a few stops with the player.
- At each stop he shares one real piece of local knowledge. For example: where the spring used to run before the road, where turtles crossed in June, which field flooded in 1977. Each one points at something real on the plot.
- He gives no quests and has no needs; he only adds to the history records. If the player isn't at the RV, he leaves a note and comes back next season.

## Set aside, and why

| Idea | Why it was set aside |
| --- | --- |
| Hunger and cooking | Now adopted as an option: food and sleep are a player setting (off, comfort or real), so it is never a forced chore |
| Foraging for food or sale | Selling wild plants works against the restoration fantasy. Small snack harvests (ramps, blackberries, chanterelles) are in the food and sleep option; seed is taken only for the nursery |
| Hunting or fishing to keep | No killing, per the canon. Catch-and-release covers the hands-on side |
| Building a cabin or base | The RV is home, by design. Comfort comes from the specimen shelf and upgrades instead |
| Rival land owners or a villain | It would make the game about conflict. The storyteller already supplies tension without one |
| Pets beyond the dog | Working animals (goats, mule, bees) are in the Tame and working animals tab; pets kept only for company are set aside |
| Multiplayer co-op | Solo for now, as decided. Saved for later |
