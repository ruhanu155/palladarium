# Tame and working animals

## Rules

| Kind | Examples | Can the player own it? | How the player gets close |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Working and domestic animals | Field dog, goats, a pack mule, honeybees | Yes | Bought or adopted through the RV computer |
| Wild animals | Everything else | Never | Habituation: calm, repeated visits lower their wariness (Mobs tab) |
| Lab animals | Wild animals in care, captive-bred stock | Held under permit, then released or placed | As in the Capture tab |

Wild animals are never tamed, fed or kept as pets. Feeding wildlife makes it dependent and spreads disease, so the game doesn't offer it, apart from the bird feeder, which follows the bear rules.

Owned animals help with real tasks: finding, grazing, hauling, pollinating. They also make the station feel like a home. An animal in care never dies. Neglect lowers its condition, and when care slips, it becomes unwilling or less effective until cared for again. Below 20 condition, the state wildlife vet collects it, or the animal's owner program takes it back, and you lose 10 reputation. Honeybee colonies may abscond (leave) rather than die.

## The field dog

A rescue dog trained to find wildlife by scent, like the real detection dogs used to find box turtles and rare plants. Adopted for $150 at Volunteer tier. The player names it.

**Commands** (a whistle item, or keys):

| Command | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Heel | Walks at your side |
| Stay | Waits where it is; use it near nests and at the pond edge |
| Search | Quarters the ground ahead for the chosen scent target, then sits by the find and looks back at you |
| Quiet | Lies down and stays still; use it for watching wildlife |
| Home | Returns to its bed in the RV |

**Scent training.** The dog starts knowing one target: eastern box turtle. Each new target needs a scent kit ($25, bought) and 3 practice sessions in the yard (a short find-the-scent game). Targets the dog can learn:

- box turtle
- red-backed salamander (under cover)
- fox or bear scat
- invasive plant patches (stiltgrass)
- a named animal's trail

**What it adds.** Searches raise survey effort for its target, so estimates come faster and tighter. The dog finds things the player would walk past. It is also good company: it greets you at the RV door, sleeps by the bed, shakes off water after wading, and its ears go up when it hears something before you do.

**Rules near wildlife.**

- On the lead (an item), it never disturbs anything.
- Off the lead within 16 blocks of a nest, den or calling pond, it scares wildlife, and the reputation cost is the same as disturbing it yourself.
- It never chases, catches or harms an animal. On a find, it sits.
- In nesting season, the briefing reminds you to keep it on the lead in the field.

## Goats for targeted grazing

Goats love the plants the cove has too much of: multiflora rose, blackberry, Japanese honeysuckle and Chinese privet. Real land managers use goats this way.

**Getting them.** Rent a herd of 4 for 7 days ($200), or buy 2–6 at Technician tier ($120 each).

**Setting up.**

- A portable electric fence makes a paddock of up to 20 × 20 blocks. The fence and its solar charger are bought; the posts are crafted from sticks.
- The goats need a water trough filled from the pump, and a small three-sided shed, crafted from planks.

**How grazing works.**

- Goats eat shrubs and vines first, and grasses and flowers last.
- Inside the paddock, invasive cover drops each day.
- After about 3 days they start on the natives too, so the paddock must be moved.
- Moving the paddock is a hands-on job: pull posts, re-set the fence, lead the goats.

**The payoff and the trade-off.** Goats clear brush on slopes too steep for the brush cutter, with no fuel and no noise. But they pack soil where they stand and eat natives if left too long. The Park Service rewards a cleared invasive patch only if it's replanted with natives within a season.

Goats never butt the player (the vanilla goat's ram is turned off). They only escape a paddock whose fence charger has a flat battery.

## The pack mule

The cove is steep. The wheelbarrow slows to a crawl on slopes steeper than 1 up per 4 across, and machines can't reach the gully's upper end or the cove head. A pack mule can.

- **Getting it.** Buy one at Volunteer tier ($400), or rent it for $30 a day.
- **Carrying.** Leads on a rope (the lead item) and carries 2 pack saddlebags of 32 blocks each, any material.
- **Ground.** Walks steep, rocky and muddy ground at full speed. Crosses the Run at fords. Leaves only light hoof marks: it packs soil only where it walks often, so a mule trail is worth placing on purpose.
- **Care.** Grazes on its own in Bench Field. Needs a water trough and a crafted lean-to. Brushing it each day keeps it willing.

In Minecraft, the mule is the vanilla mule with its chest storage, given the saddlebag model and the Field Station lead and care rules.

## Honeybees

A honeybee hive is a working animal with real trade-offs.

**Setup.** Buy a hive box and a starter colony ($180, Technician), plus a smoker, veil and hive tool ($40). Place the hive on a stand at the Bench Field edge, facing the morning sun.

**Inspections.** Every few game days, open the hive with the smoker going: a short hands-on job of lifting frames one by one.

- The player looks for the queen, eggs, capped honey and mites. Each frame shows its state clearly.
- A healthy hive grows; a hive with high mites needs a treatment ($15) or it weakens.
- Rushing an inspection without smoke riles the bees: they buzz around you for 5 seconds; no chase, no energy cost, never damage.

**Honey.** In late summer, harvest the extra frames. Honey sells through orders, about $8 a jar, capped like other sales. Always leave enough for the bees' winter.

**Swarms.** In late spring, a strong hive may swarm. The briefing warns a day ahead: crowded frames and queen cells. A swarm hanging in a tree is an opportunity: catch it in an empty box for a second hive, or lose it to the woods.

**Bears.** Bears love hives. Without an electric fence around the hive ($60), a bear will wreck it in a poor mast year, and the colony absconds to the woods.

**The native bee balance.** Honeybees compete with native bees for flowers. The cove holds at most 2 hives before the native bee allotment starts to fall, and the Park Service flags it. Good pollination for the meadow (each hive adds +0.2 to the pollination multiplier, 1.5× → 1.7×, within 32 blocks) has to be weighed against the native bees.

In Minecraft, this builds on the vanilla bee and beehive: the inspection screen, mites and the native-bee rule are added.

## Wild regulars

Wild animals can't be tamed, but some become *regulars* that let you close because you've been calm around them for a long time. This is habituation (Mobs tab) carried far.

| Regular | How it happens | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Run heron | Watch it fish from the same bank, quietly, for many mornings | It lets you within 8 blocks instead of 16; you can photograph a strike |
| A Carolina wren | It nests in a crevice of the RV awning or the chimney | It sings from the RV roof each morning; the nest is a field moment |
| Eastern phoebes | They nest under the culvert or log bridge | They return to the same nest every spring |
| The Bench Field box turtle | Found near the same spot over a season | Reliably found at the field edge after rain |
| Barred owls | Call-back surveys over a year | They answer from the same tree; one may perch near the RV at dusk |
| Chickadees and titmice | Watch the feeder in winter | They come close and scold you |

Regulars lose their trust if the player chases them, catches them more than once a season, or lets the dog run at them. A regular is often also a named animal.

## Care and costs

| Animal | Daily care | Weekly cost | When care slips |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Field dog | Feed, water, a pat; time off-duty | $10 food | Lazy searches and stays by the RV; never runs off or dies |
| Goats (each) | Water; move the paddock every 3 days | $5 minerals | They break out to graze natives or sulk in the shed |
| Pack mule | Water; brush | $8 feed in winter | Refuses to carry until brushed and watered |
| Honeybee hive | Inspect every few days in season | $5 treatment as needed | The colony weakens and may abscond to the woods |

Care jobs are quick, under a minute each, and the morning briefing lists any that are due. The 5-minute rule applies: no owned animal makes the player wait or repeat a long chore.

## Tame animals in Minecraft

| Animal | Built on | What we add |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field dog | Vanilla tamed wolf (sit, follow) with a new dog model; all vanilla wolf target and attack goals removed | The search command, scent targets, the lead rules, the RV bed |
| Goats | Vanilla goat | Ram turned off; grazing that reads the plant layers; paddock fence block with a charger |
| Pack mule | Vanilla mule and its chest | Saddlebag model and capacity, slope speed, mule-trail compaction |
| Honeybees | Vanilla bee and beehive; vanilla bee anger, stinging and death-after-sting removed | Inspection screen, mites, swarming, the native-bee rule, bear raids |

Owned animals are saved with the plot, keep their names, and travel in the trailer if the RV ever moves. They don't count toward the wildlife cap but are included in the 3 ms wildlife budget.
