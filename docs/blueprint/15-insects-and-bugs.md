# Insects and bugs

## Why bugs matter

Insects are most of the animal life in the cove and most of its food. Every habitat record keeps an **insect food base**: a number for how many insects it produces each day, split into flying, ground and aquatic. That one number feeds almost everything else:

- Birds, bats, frogs, salamanders, fish and spiders eat from it.
- Pollinators set seed for flowers.
- Decomposers turn leaves and logs into soil.

| Raises the food base | Lowers it |
| --- | --- |
| Native flowers from spring to fall | Mowing everything at once |
| Leaf litter and logs | Bare soil; jumping worms |
| Wet meadow and clean streams | Silt and warm water (aquatic insects) |
| Dark nights | Bright lights all night |
| Host plants (milkweed, spicebush, violets, oaks) | Invasive plant monocultures |

Oaks support far more caterpillars than any other trees in the region, and those caterpillars feed nearly every nestling songbird. So a planted oak is, in game terms, bird food.

## Bug tools and catching

Insects are caught, identified, photographed and released, never killed or pinned. The bug guide fills from live animals.

| Tool | Use | How it plays | Price and tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sweep net | Sweep through tall grass | Swing back and forth while walking; each sweep catches 0–5 small insects into the net | $20, Volunteer |
| Aerial net | Catch a single flying insect | Track a butterfly or dragonfly and swing as it slows; a timing ring like the dip net | $25, Volunteer |
| Beating sheet | Shake shrubs | Hold the sheet under a branch and tap; caterpillars and beetles drop onto it | Craft: 2 sticks, 1 cloth (bought) |
| Pitfall trap | Ground beetles, spiders | A cup sunk level with the ground; check within 24 hours. An unchecked trap releases its catch at 24 hours. Nothing dies in a trap | Craft: 1 jar, 1 stick |
| Moth sheet and UV light | Night moths | Hang the sheet and switch on the light; moths arrive over an hour | $60, Volunteer; uses power |
| Bug jar | Hold one insect briefly | Shows it close up for ID and photos; release within 5 minutes, or it flies free on its own | $5 |
| Hand lens | Identify small things | Zooms on anything held or under the crosshair | $10 |

**Identifying.** A caught insect opens a small ID card: two or three clear features to check (wing pattern, antennae, legs), then pick the match from a short list. Getting it right logs the species. Getting it wrong logs only the group ("a beetle"), with no penalty.

## Warmth and timing

Insects are cold-blooded, so warmth decides when they appear. Each insect group adds up **warmth points** each day (degrees above 10 °C), the way real entomologists use degree-days. A group emerges when its total passes its threshold.

| Group | Warmth points to emerge | Shows in the cove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mourning cloak butterfly | 0 (overwinters as an adult) | First warm days of spring |
| Mason bees | 2 | Early spring, with red maple flowers (about spring day 3) |
| Mayflies | 8 | Late spring evenings (spring days 5–6) |
| Synchronous fireflies | 12 | Late spring nights (spring days 6–7) |
| Common fireflies | 30 | Summer evenings at field edges (summer day 2) |
| Monarchs (arrival) | 20 | Early summer (summer day 1) |
| Katydids | 70 | Late summer nights (summer days 5–6) |

Warmth points are the sum of each day's mean temperature above 10 °C, counted from spring day 1. The thresholds are calibrated to the normal year in the Biomes tab. GameTest `fieldstation:insects/emergence_normal_year`: under the Biomes normal year, each group emerges within ±1 day of the dates above.

A warm spring brings everything early, and a cold one late. Because birds and fish time their own breeding to the season, an early or late year can put them out of step with their food. The nature calendar shows it, and the player can watch it happen.

**Daily activity.** Below 10 °C insects barely move. Butterflies need sun to fly. Rain grounds most flyers. Wind above 25 km/h keeps them low in the grass.

## Rearing

**Caterpillars to butterflies.** A rearing cage in the lab ($30, Volunteer) holds up to 5 caterpillars of one species.

1. Find caterpillars on their host plant with the beating sheet or by searching leaves: monarchs on milkweed, swallowtails on spicebush.
2. Feed them fresh host leaves daily from your own plants. Caterpillars in care never die: a missed feeding pauses their growth and lowers their condition, and below 20 condition the state wildlife vet collects them and you lose 10 reputation.
3. They pupate after about 3 game days and emerge about 3 days later.
4. Release the adult where you found it.

Reared butterflies survive better than wild caterpillars, which are often eaten. Watching a chrysalis open is a field moment.

**Monarch tagging.** In fall, migrating monarchs can be caught with the aerial net and given a tiny wing sticker, as in the real tagging programs. Each tagged monarch has a 1 in 100 chance of being "recovered" in Mexico that winter. When it happens, a postcard arrives in the RV computer's Returns inbox with the distance it flew, about 2,500 km (Capture and animal trade → Tags that travel): the cove's link to a continent-wide story. Tagging needs the collecting permit and counts toward science credits.

## Moth nights and light

**Moth nights.** On warm, still, cloudy nights, hang the moth sheet at a woods edge and switch on the UV light. Moths arrive over the next hour and settle on the sheet, and the player identifies them by hand lens. A luna moth on the sheet is a field moment. The number of kinds seen in an hour gives a **moth diversity score**, a quick night-time health check for the woods. Switch the light off after an hour so the moths can leave.

**Light at night.** Artificial light pulls in and wears out insects, and it breaks firefly signals.

| Light | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| RV outdoor light left on | Within 32 blocks: the night insect food base drops 30%, fireflies stop flashing, bats feed elsewhere |
| Moth sheet for one hour | No lasting harm |
| Red headlamp | No effect |
| White headlamp | Small effect where it points |

A motion-sensor switch for the RV light ($15) fixes the outdoor light.

### Dark sky

**Version:** v0.4.

**How it plays.** The cove's night sky is a resource. Every outdoor light lowers a dark-sky score. Warm light costs less than cool white, and a shielded light that points down costs least. A dark cove keeps fireflies flashing, moths on the sheet and bats over the pond, and it opens the Milky Way and the meteor showers (Weather and climate). Hold the score high for a whole season and the cove becomes a dark-sky site.

**The score.** `dark_sky_score`, 0–100, is recomputed each game hour from 20:00 to 06:00. It starts at the cove's ceiling of 95 (a faint glow from valley towns; about Bortle class 3, a real rural Blue Ridge sky) and subtracts each lit outdoor light's penalty. A light counts when it is on, open to the sky (no solid block within 4 above), and inside the plot or its 32-block margin.

| Light | Examples | Penalty | Insect pull radius |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cool white, unshielded | The RV outdoor light as delivered; glowstone, sea lantern, froglight | 20 | 32 blocks |
| Warm, unshielded | Torch, lantern, campfire; the RV light with a warm bulb | 10 | 24 blocks |
| Cool white, shielded | The RV light with a light shield | 8 | 12 blocks |
| Warm, shielded | Shielded lamp; the RV light with shield and warm bulb | 3 | 8 blocks |
| RV windows, blinds open | Interior light (use a window to close the blinds) | 4 | 8 blocks |
| White headlamp | Only while it is on | 2 | 8 blocks where it points |
| Red lamp or red headlamp | — | 0 | 0 |

A light on the motion switch counts 25% of its penalty and pulls insects only while it is lit. The moth sheet's UV light counts 0 during its survey hour. Within each light's pull radius, the effects in the Light at night table apply (that table's 32-block row is the cool-white case): the night insect food base drops 30%, fireflies stop flashing and bats feed elsewhere.

**Score bands.**

| Score | Band | Effect across the plot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90–95 | Dark-sky | Full Milky Way; meteor showers at full rate; the synchronous firefly night at full display |
| 70–89 | Rural | Faint Milky Way; meteors at 0.6 |
| 40–69 | Glow | No Milky Way; meteors at 0.3; night insect food base −10%; moth diversity score −15% |
| 0–39 | Bright | Meteors at 0.1; night insect food base −20%; moth diversity score −30%; no synchronous firefly night |

**Dark-sky site.** At the season report, if the score was 90 or more at 22:00 on all 7 nights of that season, the cove earns the badge: +30 reputation, 8 SC and a dark-sky plaque for the RV. The badge stays. If a later season has 3 or more nights below 90, the plaque reads "lapsed" until a clean season; there is no reputation loss.

**Watching the sky.** Place the star blanket on open ground and use it to lie back. The camera tilts up and the HUD fades; hold use to time-lapse at 4×, and get up any time. It is a rest: in Comfort mode it restores 4 energy per game hour (a third of the RV bed's 12). In Real mode it restores 2 and does not count as sleep. With food and sleep Off it is just the view. Counting meteors is in Weather and climate → The night sky.

**Mistakes.** A light left on costs that night's insects, fireflies and bats near it, and some score. It all comes back the next dark night. The score never costs money or items.

**Hooks.** Light at night (above); moth nights and the moth diversity score; fireflies and the synchronous firefly night (Fun, feel and finish event catalog); bats and the insect food base (Mobs); the night sky (Weather and climate); the RV outdoor light (Lab and RV); the season report; the morning briefing ("The RV light was on all night").

**GameTests.**

- `fieldstation:insects/dark_sky_score_counts_lights`: one unshielded cool-white light on at 22:00 gives 75; adding a light shield gives 87; putting it on the motion switch gives 93.
- `fieldstation:insects/shield_restores_fireflies`: fireflies 20 blocks from the bare RV light do not flash; after the light shield is fitted (radius 12), they flash the same night.

## The bug guide

A section of the field journal for insects, built from live catches and photos. It holds 40 insect species in v1.0, grouped as in the table above; more come with later biomes.

Each page shows:

- a photo slot
- the ID features
- host plants
- the season and time of day it appears
- where the player found it
- one plain fact

Completing a group, such as all the cove's dragonflies, earns a little reputation and science credit. The guide also feeds species bingo and field moments, if those concept ideas are adopted.

## Bugs in Minecraft

- **Swarms are particles.** Most insects are particle effects driven by the food base numbers, as in the Mobs tab: drifting midges, bees on flowers, fireflies, mayfly hatches.
- **A few catchable insects.** Within 16 blocks of the player, the game spawns up to 8 small insect entities: a butterfly, a dragonfly, a beetle on a log. These are the ones the nets catch, and they count toward the 64-entity wildlife cap (Mobs tab). Their species is drawn from what the habitat holds, in proportion.
- **Sweep and beat catches** don't need entities: the catch is rolled from the habitat's insect mix when the net is used.
- **Caught insects are items** with data components (species, where caught), like caught animals in the Capture tab.
- **Models** are tiny and simple, readable mostly by color and wing shape. Clean, never creepy.
- **Cost.** The food base is part of each habitat record, updated with the hourly ecology step. The degree-day totals are one small number per group.
